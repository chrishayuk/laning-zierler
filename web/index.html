Instrumentation Laboratory · Massachusetts Institute of Technology · Engineering Memorandum E-364

A Program for Translation of Mathematical Equations for Whirlwind I

J. H. Laning Jr. and N. Zierler, January 1954, reconstructed and running in this page

“The effect of our program is to create a computer within a computer.” You typed your problem on a Flexowriter as ordinary equations, the punched tape went into Whirlwind’s reader, every equation was translated into a block of floating-point subroutine calls on the magnetic drum, and the answers came back typed. Type a tape on the left and feed it.

Every equation ends with a comma, a PRINT with a period, and the program with STOP. Juxtaposition is multiplication; a space between two numbers is too (2 3 is 6). Exponents were upper-case digits: type x^2, or Alt with a digit for x². F²(x) is sin x. And a slash does not divide by the next factor alone: a/b/c is ac over b.

Before anything ran, the whole tape was translated. Each equation became a block of calls on the floating-point
          interpretive routines, kept on the drum and brought into core one equation at a time. Equations the programmer did not number
          were numbered from 101, “in the order in which they are written”. (These orders are the reconstruction’s; Whirlwind’s are not known.)

        

      

      

        
Each equation as it was carried out, with the value stored; each jump, and whether it was taken.
          Passes through the D equations are numbered: Gill’s method goes through them four times for each step.

        

      

      

        
What the run cost, counted. The system ran “about six to eight times as long” as Whirlwind’s ordinary
          interpretive system (MIT summer session, 1954), “of the order of magnitude of ten to one from an efficient computer program” (C. W. Adams, May 1954; Laning is named as co-author but did not write it).
          The one cost E-364 gives in time is the drum: a variable there takes “about 30 milliseconds longer”.

        

      

      

        
Whirlwind was a one’s-complement machine: “0 may appear as +0 or −0” (E-364, p. 8). CP jumps on −0, and a
          superscript may be zero only “if assigned by the specific equation n = 0,” (p. 13). The same tape, fed twice, once with each zero.
